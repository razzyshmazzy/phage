---
title: "Borderline Kraken"
date: 2026-07-28
category: poetry
---

The pictures, azure pockets of memory. Tumultuous tides and a ravaged sea. Blasphemous rocking upon the ocean waves. A boat of long wooden planks, stitched together amateurishly. A crew of rugged children, sails bolstered upon their hearts. A voyage destined for failure.

Many a night, many a man. A cabbage soup and a sailor's hand. They were poor, they were. A boiling cesspool of carrot nor sand. It was marvelous, really, that their stomachs did not eat themselves, tired of long stints of endless hunger. Long time had gone, the sails with keep upon their hearts began to waver, the wind, their will and guide, beginning to die out.

A flame of sorrow. Not red, but blue and black. Extraordinarily bright.

Morbid, an ember of sentimental discussion. Rosaries upon rosaries of lies. A tailcoat jacket with buttons sowed meticulously. A cap with a feather through its roof. Half a mind to tell off the mass. Roars of orders spat to the sky. Heavens above knew of him, the captain of the azure galleon.

Though a sloop in his heart.

Never mind that. He was washed over by the smashing waters, a bluish green. Wet and uncomfortable. A chilly breeze splashed against his face, and the water was blown off. His hair was glued to his face grossly, covering his eyes. He swept it away with a cold finger.

The wood beneath his feet felt incredibly sturdy. Oak. Such a grand being. Those tall and sturdy oaks — his ship was crafted from such a mighty being. Alas, the waves seemed its bane. They crashed along its stern and its bow in equal, slamming the ship from tide to tide. A horrible storm of blows, leaving white marks down the ship's sides.

His face, marked by uncertainty, but lest did he allow it to show. Morale would dip, and that was key during these tumultuous times.

Steadfast his iron tendons were, tensing and loosening like a game of Tug-O-War. His eyes were a steely blue, and the oceans borne from his gaze held vises on his men. Metallic allure, a bronze wristwatch. Five to six. Heavens be to his side, his wretched teeth and malnourished hips.

The wind caught the sails and the vessel soared.

“Save our souls, she would ya lass!” the captain roared with renewed vigor. The ship rocked jovially in response, a stray spotlight falling upon the mast.

Again, he regaled, “Heathens be, a new fish we shall find and with it we shall catch.” “Yarrr!” the crew cried. Pirates they were indeed.

And so the boat creaked, misty-eyed, it went. It lunged forward, breaking through the gales. The crew was bathed in a dewy glow as the sun broke through. A sight for eyes sore.

Stomachs so tangled let go their locks, and deposited their insides overboard. Eyes stood childlike upon their captain, trust stalwart. For his mettle was that which was dipped in glory, the deepest molten core. A star, he was, amidst the roaming seas.

“Golden sea dog!” they would cry. “Oh, captain, my captain!”

His chest, though meager and scrawny, was a metallic synergy of gold and silver. He was their guiding light, shining through blinding waves of blue and white. Their praise reached his ears, and he smiled larger than a chest of gold could open, lips blue and black — touched by that same sorrowful flame.

Suddenly, the boat lurched and a fog dropped.

“Land ho!” they all cheered.

Their boots crammed together before the bulwark as they launched from the ship, landing delicately in the hot sand. They left marks as they trudged up the shore, into the green thicket. Navy flowers wrapped around the trees in poisonous jubilee, tempting hands to their death-claiming bodies. One idiot tried his luck with the plant; oh, how hungry he was. His veins bulged and a surge of fear shot through him. Just as the captain, last to leave the boat as they do, touched upon the sandy shore, the crew mate fell ill and dropped to the forest floor. His extremities were blacked and disgusting. Quickly, his former friends rolled him to the side, pushing him into a hole. One or two shed a tear, alas he was an idiot, so many figured this end just.

How funny it is that death comes quickly for some, yet waits a lifetime for others.

Only the azure memories, their deep pockets. Only justice prevailed when judged. At least that was what the captain sought. But neither heaven nor heathen carried worthy answers. Neither ship nor sail, or annihilation upon the great blue — a seaworthy vessel caked in white crustaceans hanging by a thread.

Docking their large ship, the men shivered as night approached. They worked quickly to design a fire and a warm place to rest. The bushes rumbled with anticipation, and all eyes shot to them immediately. It was a bunny, white as night, with eyes red. Lusting for blood. Or was it they who carried such a lust? Did it matter?

The answer: it did not. They pounced on it immediately, as if wild beasts. A savage image of locked arms and desperate claws was painted. The bunny had become contested territory, its red eyes flashing a somber blue as its life faded into nothing.

A few moments into the clambering mess of starvation, a voice rang out, instantly bringing the sound to a lull. "Gentlemen!” the captain shouted. "We may be pirates with a lust for savagery, gold and all sorts of other delicious treasures running through our veins. But we are not animals. Now, quit this fighting and mend the fire!”

With a violent gesture and a raised tone, the men hurriedly dismounted each other. Though that savage light remained, a hidden ruby beneath their irises, the men knew not to disobey their captain. Even if their stomachs wanted to.

The captain nodded. His palms stuck into the sand and he pushed himself up from beside the fire, walked around it, and kneeled beside the massacred chunks of the late bunny. His face was expressionless.

He picked up a piece of it. Flesh matted with fresh blood which drizzled past his thumb. Cold.

Soft, too.

Fur white as snow.

He held the sorry bunny's remains for a moment more. He could not help but think of what it would feel like to be dogpiled by a crew of disheveled men, crushed beneath their weight and ripped to pieces beneath their claws. It must feel horrible — it must have felt horrible.

He sighed, closing his eyes.

It was unimportant now, he supposed.

Suddenly, a bony hand poked his shoulder, spooking him. "Cap’n,” the hand said, “we are hungry.”

The captain lowered his hand and placed the bunny's flesh on the ground. “Of course. Gather 'round, and we shall cook this bunny. First come, first serve.”

And so they did as such.

Alas, there was little food to gain from a slaughtered bunny, and only a quarter of the crew had been fed by the end. The captain scratched his chin in thought. Stomachs groaned painfully around him.

Making up his mind, he looked to his crew, and spoke, “Those that have been fed," he pointed to the depths of the forest, unlit by the fiery glow of the campfire, “are to follow me into the woods. We will acquire rations for the rest of the crew.” The quarter that had been fed nodded. Though, a few did whisper some denials or try to hide, but those few quickly had a change of mind when the captain's fist raised before their face. "Ye were fed, time to feed those who havn't such a luxury. We go at once.”

The captain's boots left sandy imprints leading into the dark forest. Branches hung low overhead, and were nearly caught upon by each member of the expedition. Some tripped on the low roots that wiggled beneath their feet as if alive. Some were frightened by the darkness of the forest, yet followed their captain anyway.

It was not long before they reached a clearing. Upon arrival, the captain turned around and spoke to the men, “Split and look for food. Happen upon a peach tree or perhaps one of coconut, alert me of its location.”

A calm gesture later, the men had found the tree. It was not too large, but it was one that would feed the entire crew, if not for a few nights. One man stuffed a peach into his pocket and made off into the shadow of the night with it. His absence was not recorded, and his disappearance went unnoticed. He would be glad he did.

The captain looked for a moment before spying a green rope. Some swinging vine. He yanked it from a branch, though with some effort, and tied it around the peach tree. Tugging once, he saw that it was held firm. Even if stormy bellows threatened the tree, the rope would hold. Satisfied, he led his men back through the forest. Crickets chirped in their ears, and frogs echoed their “ribbits” through the calm branches. The moon shone brightly above.

A calm blue with a white lens.

It looked like a gold coin, or a wheel of cheese, maybe?

The men stepped past the last ingrown root and reached the campfire once more.

The captain rubbed his eyes tiredly, releasing a yawn. "Those who are hungry," he yawned again, “follow me.”

The forest shook suddenly. Violently. The captain nearly lost his balance.

“Wha—“

He looked back. His eyes widened. It was a scene he would never forget.

Large grotesque tentacles lashed out from the sea. They dwarfed even his largest man in size. Stunned silent, the captain could only watch in shock as a different tentacle grabbed each one of his men, dragging them into the black abyss.

Their faces carried fear. Deep surprise scarred their final moments.

Below, the sea churned with maniacal fury. A storm approached before he could react. Rain slammed against his back, but he remained in place. He looked back warily. Though the fire was extinguished, he could still see.

But what did it matter to see if there was nothing to look at?

Saliva packed together and pushed down his throat.

Empty.

That was the only word that came to mind. His entire crew had disappeared, and he had a good idea as to where. He gazed at the black depths silently. He still had not eaten, but he could not care less. His mind was completely focused on one thing:

The image of those daunting tentacles, large enough to capsize a boat.

This thought led him to check, and yes, his ship had also disappeared. The culprit was obvious. Still, just what, or who, was the culprit?

It had... tentacles. So, octopus? Squid?

"Giant squid…” he muttered indistinctly, teeth chattering madly.

A songbird sang from the murky forest depths, yet its beautiful melody disdained touch upon his ears.

Tweet, tweet.

Night came and went silently, without argument, yet he remained stationary. Unmoving, but constantly thinking. His boots were bolted to the ground tightly. His thoughts slammed him from all sides, and the harrowing image continued to resurface as if haunting him. He hated it.

He hated it so much.

So.

Damn.

Much.

His eyes filled with water, and his tears finally began to shed. Penance was long overdue. Painfully, he mourned the loss of his crew. It hit him hard, harder than anything had ever hit him. Why, just why. was he cursed to this fate?

Why did the tentacle leave?

Why didn't it take him with it?

His nose twitched as memory-laden tears trickled over, falling off its bridge. He sniffled. Tears like salt, traveling past his chin, down his neck, over his gold-embedded shark tooth necklace, down his hairy torso, his scarred chest, over his prominent ribs, his bony waist and protruding hips, until finally trailing down his pant legs and dribbling into the yellow sand, painting it brown. His tears were cold, blue. They were the sky, they were the sea.

They were azure.

Pockets of memory held in their aquatic glow.

Glistening pictures.

A crew and their captain.
