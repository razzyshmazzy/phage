---
title: "Of Ash and Fire"
date: 2026-07-28
category: poetry
year: freshman
---

I stumble through familiar streets, unable to make out the hazy features before me. Swathes of black ash coat the asphalt, leaving prints of my feet as I move past; like lost memories, soon swept away. Heavy soot swarms the sky and fills my lungs, forcing me to heave out heavy breaths every few moments.

Caught in one such sudden bout, I clench my chest as I fall to my knees, feeling a burning sensation welling up inside. My eyes widen in anticipation, and I seize my throat. Animalistic growls dwelling deep within me find themselves without the sufficient force to pry themselves free. The passage of time wanes, wavers, falls, and fails, slowing to crawl; and then, further, as the world's maw closes upon my agony, and time slows to a complete halt.

And then, in every fear of mine found out, explored, and exploited, and every finite desire of mine forever buried, the ugly face of pain rears its repulsive head, and burrows its claws deep within my solar plexus. I heave emptiness out my bleeding gut. I hear my own heart, beating within my chest. I whine. My whole body seizures and spasms in that moment, when time begins to walk once more. Clouds of black dust and burning ash, as if personifications of my hellish pain, pour from out my profaned mouth in violent coughs, returning to their home in the poisoned air.

Ill, I fall to my stomach, my eyes glazing over with pale despair. There is only despair in this rapidly descending darkness, for hope has long since been cured from my mind. And glad it is, I bare my teeth madly and groan harshly as I press my palms against the heated asphalt, raising my body from the soot.

I stagger to my feet in a daze, and wipe my mouth with a forearm. I can see nothing in this red haze, save that white, sovereign light at the end of the street. Hope, like desire, has long since left me. I am bereft of it! And yet, I should crawl so desperately for safety, as if it were the curse of humanity to desire survival to such a degree—of this race, I, therefore, press against the bounds of logic, and beyond.

Pulling my weary legs to attention, I force them forward for one last push. Agitated, the red-hot sun sears into my skin, boiling it, and leaving disgusting pustules in its wake.

I do not scream in pain; I do not even whimper. I am long past these easing mercies. Instead, I delegate every fiber of my being to this timeless struggle. Growing ignorant to my surroundings, I see before me only that one light, that saving grace, that infinite mercy, that heaven-sent forgiveness.

But I have no legs, no skin, no muscles—no sinews. My bodily form is bereft of autonomy. And yet I see my foot placed before the next, again, and again, and again. As if being controlled by another, I have lost all feelings of proprioception—perhaps it is a mercy, a blessing. I breathe in the rank air. I smell nothing. It is a mercy, I confess, to be devoid of all feeling in a world of torturous pain. This ravenous hell, though its trials, has stolen from me my happiness, and in the same motion, my despair.

Reaching the end of the street, I come to an unbalanced halt. The light is before me, I realize. Motes of ash fall upon my pupil, agitating it—I do not blink. I almost forget to breathe, only when asphyxiation causes my gaze to blur and blacken—and I lose sight of the light—do I remember the motions.

I stumble forward, and am consumed by the burning light. Suddenly, I am awash in vigor, standing alone in a room of white walls and dirty, yellow light. Liquid life pours over me, revitalizing my body, and reviving my soul. I can think, I realize after a while, without the rhythm of pain playing in the background, as has plagued me for so long.

Curious, I look down at my ash-stained hands, only to find them smooth and peachy. Marveling at the sight, I clench them into two fists, feeling my sinews contract, and my muscles bulge, for the first time in months. An unconscious laugh, a dull chuckle, sounds from out my mouth, and I wonder if that clean tone came from me. I laugh again, this time on purpose. I hear it reverberate in the white room, and cannot believe my ears, but also cannot deny what I hear. I am laughing! Laughing! And so I laugh again, and again, and again, and again, until I am laughing at the top of my lungs, as if screaming. I laugh as loud as I can, laughing louder than the last laugh each time, as if competing with myself. And, as I do, my mind is totally blank.

I think nothing. I know nothing.

I am wholly insane. And I embrace my insanity.

Suddenly, the hidden cuckoo clock rings from inside the walls, and fear enslaves me. I wrap my hands around my chest, then look down to find them bound. I struggle, but the clock rings again, disorienting me. That alarm, that alarm, that horrible, horrible, four-armed alarm.

My subsequent laughs are caught in my throat. Usurping the throne of insanity, I shiver and throw my back against the padded wall. No! No! My eyes leap around the room, this terribly-cramped space, scanning for any sign… any horrible, hellish sign…

But I find none.

After a few minutes, I let out a shaky exhale, and blink in relief. As I open my eyes, I feel his arms wrap around me from behind, and his face bury itself into my back. I feel his mouth press against me, his lips moving in unheard words. And then I am pulled back into the abyss. I claw for the light, but am mercilessly swallowed into the hole in the white wall.

The scene changes three times as I fall, each a hazy blur, until I find myself somewhere familiar. Faced with cold reality, the endless cycle, I forget myself—no, I choose to forget myself—and am, again, imbued with that same curiosity. In this place, I stumble through familiar streets, unable to make out the hazy features before me.

Suddenly, I stop. My mouth opens to speak. I can still feel it at this point. “More mechanic than flesh, more robot than human," I say, watching swathes of black ash coat the asphalt.

Emotion is dead sentiment before me, I confess, leaving prints of my feet as I move past.

Like lost memories, soon swept away, I am apathetic to my suffrage.
