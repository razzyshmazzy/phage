---
title: "Paradise"
date: 2026-07-28
category: poetry
---

High — high, high up! — a cloud of misty white floats across the gleaming ocean. It paints a visage of a grand ship performing a voyage just as grand beneath the warm sun's glow. Fluffy and pristine, an untouched relic, the lonesome cloud sleeps soundly up there on its perch.

Up, higher, on the back of this cloud, some patch of greenery claims the spot. In the middle, full of radiant sparkling, a small spring sits calmly. On its banks there is no trampled grass, no flowers dug up by their roots; no mismatched terrain, neither are its proportions too great, too small, but rather perfect in size. Truly, this spring was the epitome of beautiful, even more so with the aid of the orange-colored sun, under which it shone a nice, hazy gold.

Small trees, all nearly the same size, hung not above the spring, but a small bit from its edges. They dared not encroach on water's edge, not even allowing their venomous shadows to lean upon its golden grace. Merely existing in the water's presence was enough for them. Their leaves were of a dull green, and not a single ounce of life coursed through their thick plant fibers. From a ways away, one would be hard pressed to call this image alive, yet it was not without the vibrancy of life. If anything, it seemed a dichotomy between the two. This place, it existed within a league of its own.

And what is that if not the essence of paradise?

As the cloud drifted further over, approaching a new continent, a small yellow light glinted upon the water’s surface. Yellow-white light, a splendidly royal color, exuded from the small thing resting upon the water, napping quietly. With light steps it woke, rose into the air, the flapping of its silken wings so tender, so soft. It spun and circled the island-cloud in mesmerizing arcs, as if it were a happy lark.

This being was none other than the mythical elf of legend, known only to Man by nigh forgotten lore from the times of yore, the only being of appropriate standing to reside upon this paradisiacal island. Fluttering upon the tiniest of gales, drifting from water’s edge to water’s edge, sinking beneath the cool surface and lying in the short grass — were all indulgences of this little elf. Above its illustrious skin sat a yellow cone of coalescing hair strands, bright as can be. And beneath that, a pair of eyes tinted a golden-yellow. It had a small button nose and long ears that lifted skyward, though unable to reach for any sort of paradise or heaven, as it already knew the most bountiful of them, the most beautiful and exquisite. Alas, the most frugal, too. There were no decadent halls, no purple linens nor gold galore. No haughty nobles, no snarky mistresses, and no shameless actings, as many places in this world dubbed “paradise” might require. Though many of these are those built and inhabited by Man, and Man is not known for being a peaceful society. The opposite, matter of fact. No, this paradise was merely a spring and nothing more.

Yet that is precisely it! Precisely the reason for its outstanding beauty, a radiancy the elf might bask in. The frugality of its existence, simply such a worrisome place, free of so many attachments, allowing it the light of humility and freedom. Of relaxation. Of peace.

By virtue, paradise was the escape from all chaos. And thus, the elf, on its journey continent-to-continent, found itself pondering largely on the feast surrounding it. As an elf, it need not worry about hunger, a boon of its race, and so this feast was solely spiritual, maybe even a bit philosophical. Flapping solemnly above the still waters, it would rack its brain thoughtfully, pondering, pondering slowly. Ever so slowly. So carefully, with much anticipation ringing in its bird-sized bones.

Despite being on its lonesome, it did not fret, rather such a thing allowed for a calmer collection of his thoughts. Though, to be honest, it would not have minded a companion. Conversation once a decade or so would have given it some joy. Then again, even spaced a ten year apart, this would still allow for many conversations, as elves had lifespans far longer than those of humans and other races.

Years would pass in the blink of an eye, yet the island-cloud remained mostly unchanging. Much like the elf on its surface, still pondering, pondering ever so slowly and carefully. However, it soon passed above a narrow continent, and charged into the Dark Lands. How wretched a place it was, filled with ghouls, ogres, goblins, and other menaces. The elf looked down from its perch with a narrow gaze, and sat along the edge of the island. It began to observe the proceedings below keenly. It had no need to leave the cloud and fly below to watch, something that could possibly spell its end, since elves, betraying their miniature size, had eyes that could pierce the farthest distances. Even up beside the fellow clouds, on its small, paradisiacal spring island, was it able to see down to the ground, nearly as if it was but a meter away and not thousands above.

And soon, dastardly things of dark came into view, ranging from boiling red skin to tongues of dreadful lengths, the creatures below were easily enough to fill the little elf’s heart with fear. But sat up there, upon the paradise island, it calmed so quick, as if the heated feeling had never come upon it. It watched in anticipation at the beings. It craved knowledge of all sorts, and the Dark Lands were a place most devoid of research, as the result of most expeditions usually tended to be death, even if a wizard or two were to be brought along for protection. So, with eyes focused and little pearly whites clenched, the elf began its own study.

The vileness of below reached it only in sight, but for a regal creature like an elf, that was nearly enough to send the contents of its stomach churning to the ground. It saw sights grotesque, like scavenging fiends partaking in rotting corpses of others. Sometimes it would find a sight most fear-inducing, such as when it saw the beast called Gargantuan, resting atop its hallowed out nest, in the midst of an active volcano. Routinely, it would start to spew pyres of molten red into the sky, nearly sending the elf reeling, despite not even reaching halfway between the ground and the island-cloud. Again, it would collect itself and gaze upon the Dark Lands with rapid anticipation. It saw that beast snoring soundly in the heart of the volcano, its skin not turning even the slightest bit red at the lava running around its entirety. As unbelievable as it may seem, the beast nearly did not fit in the volcano, needing to tuck its body in to just barely make it. Horribly large as it was, it became abundantly clear why so many expeditions to these Dark Lands had failed.

But that wasn’t even the worst of it.

A dark and stormy sea stirred, like the large cauldron of a witch as it went to work brewing a vile concoction, blinding the elf with its aggressive tides. The island-cloud floated just above the stormy clouds that constantly raged above this region of the Dark Lands. The elf, finding its vision obscured, decided to move into the storm to get a better look at the landscape. In its mind, there shouldn’t have been any sort of beast in the sky, never mind at the height he was at. So, it memorized the path of the island-cloud. It would be back in a few moments, minutes, maybe. Any longer would be asking for trouble. Fluttering down, the elf had to strain its little wings as it dove into the gray clouds. Deep, deeper, and deeper still, until finally it came to a place it could peer through the gray embrace, inches above where rain began to fall like bludgeoning weapons. And it was there that it heard the thunder, and from its periphery, the flash of lightning. It turned. The lightning flashed again, bluish amidst the rumbling nimbus hovering the quaking sea. The elf’s heart paled like a vampire’s, all the blood draining from its skin in an instant as it bore witness to what hid behind those ominous gray clouds. The only sound was the sonorous thunder's hammer, rushing over the elf's senses, yet unable to reach its mind. For but a second, only in that mere moment, it had seen it. Hidden deep beneath the veil of clouds, a massive silhouette that blotted out the very skies, its wings outstretched heaven-bound, seeming the width of an entire castle wall, or more — and the fear that followed, easily the worst the little one had ever experienced, wrapping around the little elf's soul like a vice, constricting its thoughts. Motionless except for the flapping of its wings, the silken things continuously beating dully against the seething storm, the elf stared blankly at where that silhouette had been. It had known at once of what it saw, yet never did it stop to think of the sheer size of these legendary behemoths. Indeed texts by Man were correct, right and just as a stern jury in calling these beasts legendary. Their title was all deserved and more. Now, seeing one such beast of magnificent tales in person, the elf would have personally dubbed them mythical, though refrained as such tag was better applied to the hidden, for races such as druids, which could be found across many forests, but only if they so elected to show themselves, and those warm hobbits with an affinity for pies (elves too, of course).

Collecting itself, the elf surged back through the storm.
