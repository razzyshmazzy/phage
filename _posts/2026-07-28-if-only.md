---
title: "If Only"
date: 2026-07-28
category: poetry
---

If only,
If only I had been born sooner.

But this is just a rumor,
Flaying my mind;
Quite possibly a tumor.

Fine!
I knew naught of history;
Still!
Anything would trump this misery.

Thus it was in the evening while I wasted
That she came to me quite late.
In a dress of flowing white
Surely it must have been Fate.

More than bright; indeed,
She was quite the sight.

I watched her figure
As her fluttering garb flowed;
She was Day’s veil
And Night’s cloak.

But just as sudden
Time slowed;
All froze.

The room warbled and spun.
She took me by my sleeve,
And through a wormhole was I wrung.

Through time did my soul affix,
Trailing this woman whom I knew only mist.

And past the skies did we soar;
Over valleys did we leap!
Till finally,
Finally we came upon what rested atop the peak.

It was the dawn of the world.
Here where dinosaurs roamed,
No longer fossilized bone
Clad in scales in this prehistoric zone.

Vividly, the great tyrannosaurus,
Who stomped the lands.
The gargantuan leviathans,
Who flooded the sands.

But the woman in white did not wait,
And quickly did she take
Dragging me up and past this
Prehistoric Dream.

We flew once more.
And my soul did it careen.
Till again was I wrung
In another era
This one of frost
And in my head was time lost

I came to in a land of ice
And in her eyes rested a question
But still I answered:
“No dice.”

Again we flew.
As time warbled,
As we fluttered through.

Firmly I remained gripped to her
Placing my life in her embrace.
I trusted her,
Though I had seen only a sliver of face.
And no sooner did we approach the next place.

We landed among rubble,
In a garden of insurmountable trouble.
Under a king of Sumerian rule,
Babylon, did we view.

And off again just as soon,
To the next Sumerian legend,
The City of Akkad
We did siege through.

Of wide seas,
He was the lord;
Great oceans did he ford,
But in spotting us as we soared,
He brandished his sword.

And if I might be bleat,
For a man of little name in fate
Little fame in face
And feats without date
He was exceptionally high on his Sumerian seat.

The first king of history
Sargon the Great;
Though his reign is shrouded in mystery
Our arrival shouldn’t be too late.

She and I stayed not a moment longer
For seconds sooner our heads should have fallen
At the feet of this warmonger,
Who charged our disappearing figures with reckless abandon.

Luckily, the rift absorbed us
And we flew through as if a race
Quickly to the next event
As sweat beaded down my face.

With all this spatial drift
It was near impossible to stay sane
But soon my spirits would lift
Till then I can only feign.

We warbled once more
And were spat suddenly
Upon the shore.

Disoriented,
I looked up from my trance
And before me glittered
A vast expanse.

The Aegean Sea.
I was entranced.

This was the first location
I felt truly free.
Here, by the Aegean Sea.

Across the blue
Its wings shot out
As early Greece
Sprouted round.

But then did I see
An army by the sea;
A legion of golden abs.
In tow a horse,
The size of a house.

They saw me not
And thank the woman in white for that
But still I wondered:
"What era was it
In that a horse that fat
Was naught but flab?”

Surely, there was something
Sneaking behind those slabs.
Voices whispering,
Drowned by the sound of marching abs.

Then I knew,
This one was too soon.

Alas,
Like the eras before
It too was
Unfit and sore.
Thus,
As before,
Indeed, to her I did implore,
That we forsake the Aegean Sea,
And continue our journey
Through Earth’s lore.

Again we were sucked
And again we were spat.

When I wrestled the stun of swapping time,
Finally did I see
In a mixture of recognization and jubilee
The sight of dreams.

Still only a century later,
Yet the lands had grown prosperous.
Ere the time of Socrates
Still there managed the essential monolith.

Truly,
A pillar of modern society,
This quickly growing city
Yes!
The Eternal City.

Rome.
The home of Earth's longest empire.

In this place of dreams,
Truly,
Everywhere I might gleam
Every sight I might savor
Was mainly full of menial labor.

Still I looked further:
I saw the science
And the culture;
I saw the architecture,
I saw the people.

Many in number were they,
Full of cheer
As they mumbled legends
The brothers Romulus and Remus
And the empire their lives did build.

But still,
I am left to think;
Because still
I stood paralyzed,
My ambitions unable to blink.

This Eternal City,
In having everything,
Still lacked the thing for me.

Truly,
What a pity.

Downtrodden,
I wallowed.

Misery was all I could expect.
I swallowed.

And the void in my heart,
Felt a a little less hollow.
For her hand was there,
On my shoulder,
She did care.
That woman clad in white
With skin more than fair.

Then,
Whisked away once more,
Centuries floated by,
And again did we soar.

Space tangled before me
And time, it did leap.
Soon my eyes reopened,
Her efforts would I reap.

I sighed at the sight
For still did we linger
In the City Eternal;
Still, behold my stupor.

For the skies did I dread then,
As red paint was flung
And ash, rock, and gray
Seized my vision
While smoke filled my lungs.
The eruption of Mount Vesuvius,
That feeling of impending doom
Truly it was the same
As witnessing nuclear fission.

But the mushroom would not touch me,
For as soon as the waves of smoke hit,
A hand latched round my wrist,
And through a wormhole was I wrung.

Only sooner than I could cough a storm
Did we stop
And before me did I witness
A thing forlorn
The Bible's greatest event,
At the sight did I stop.

Before me,
He was stabbed into a cross.
But unlike most
His soul was not yet lost.

In a field of crosses
Was his body tossed.
In a field of crosses
Did his thorny crown rest.
In a field of crosses
Did his limp head hang
At the peoples’ behest.

But I could bear the sight no longer,
So quickly as I beckoned,
Did we leave for the next location.

Indeed did we sift
Through a storm once more.
And only a millennia later
Did we finally burst out the wormhole.

My knees clashed with stone;
And twelve swords unsheathed
Prepared to cut me to bone.

But then a hand did raise
A presence which set my heart ablaze
A vigor the likes only
Merlin's chosen could attain.
The swords did re-sheath
Still, the knights were seething with fury
For only in their king's presence
Were they forced to show mercy.

I looked up;
But to my surprise,
The presence which spared me
Belonged not to prodigal fate
Twas not legendary King Arthur
But Charlemagne the Great.

The Holy Roman Emperor
And king of the Franks.

A man of visual appeal
And kingly, regal;
Near him did I feel meek
In the knees, weak.

I scrambled away
Back into the wormhole
My heart set ablaze;
As the wormhole began to fade
A thing nearly robbed me of my gaze
Twas a bolting sword of silver
Aiming for my face.

Scared,
I closed my eyes
And luckily it vanished
Without a trace.

Again through a wormhole was I wrung
Till finally I was spat out
Under the whirring sun.

In my appearing
Did I witness
A wave of light
Washing over the horizon.
The clashing of white steel
Ripping apart ligaments.

Seething, their suits gleamed
Their faith, ripe like wine
And so did their bucket helms shine
Crosses,
With their hands did they sign.

But as I stood there amidst
A battlefield heavensent,
I simply could not fathom
The point of the event.

As much as the cold night leaked
Was as much as I wished to sleep
The tidings of this time were far too wild;
I simply could not find it in me to smile.

Disinterested and disappointed
The wormhole opened for me again,
And through it I leapt
And through it was I sent.

Again time whirred to life
Carrying me through its expanse
What next would be my plight?

As time cleared for me to see
I found myself in a place serene
A place of peace
During the time of reformation
In the land of Florence, Italy.

Where Humanism was slowly becoming less moot
And the times were following suit.

I stood almost astral
Behind a man lit by oil lamp
Peering over his shoulder,
I bore witness to his plans.

As he penned a comedy
Of the divine sort.

But upon closer focus
In it did I notice
The theme mortis
And what happened post this.

Atop the papyrus
Signed Italian-stylish
The man who penned his deepest wish
Dante Alighieri
Author and political activist.

Watching his pen trace letters across the page
I felt a tad sentimental
And I thought back to prior days,
Was I truly as unhappy as I claimed?

As I thought these thoughts,
Dante turned the page
And looked over his shoulder
Just as I was whisked away.

He looked to where
I had seconds ago been
And uttered the phrase:
“Must have been the wind.”

In the wormhole I flew
The currents threw,
And at another point in history
I drew.

My eyes gleamed at the sight
Of a crow or a raven,
A man clad in black;
He looked to me
And quickly I began to act.

Over a bleeding corpse he rose
And at me he groaned.
But in all my fright and antirepose
I backed away nearly too slow.

Immediately,
The woman clad in white
Dragged me back through quickly;
Her eyes held the words of apology.

But the moment was fleeting
And luckily the Bubonic Plague had not seeped in.

Thus again was I wrung
Through a wormhole
And flung at the sun.

But I did not scare
As the wind flowed through my hair
As I was bound for the ground
I felt her gales soften the air.

Carefully,
I landed upon the Orleans fortress
And bore witness
To that young lady's courage.

Entranced,
I stayed
And soon did I see her flayed
Burned at the stake.

But at least in death
Was her name inscribed
In the tomes of the church
For which all might hark:
The saint,
Jeanne d'Arc.

Again I walked away
And through a wormhole was I wrung.

I exited just as fast
And beheld the great blue
From atop a sturdy mast.

Land ahoy
For before me, untrodden views
Suppose except for the natives
For they were here long before.

But could they win
In a war?
Because if not
Then the land was as good as ours.

And so we stepped upon the grass
Leading ourselves further past.
Columbus led the pack
And as he did, I stepped back.

Thus again was I wrung
Through a wormhole
Was I flung.

I landed in a bed of cotton;
The air smelled of nothing
My curiosity soared
And I began to suspect something.

Where had I fallen?
Or when,
I suppose.

But then when I saw
That the sheets were not made
Suddenly did I realize
That it was in my own bed that I laid.

But of absolute surprise to me
Out of all locations I had visited
Along my travels
Across the history of the human race;
It was only here
In my place
That I felt at peace,
In my bed
In the modern day.

If only,
If only I had been born sooner.
But this is just a rumor,
Flaying my mind;
Definitely, O definitely
A tumor.
