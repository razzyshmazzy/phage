## Sonnet of the Void

Was there ever something so encompassing
As that land of zero absolutes
Here I hear the bated breath of nothing
Amid all these breathless hues;
No God, I might mutter, ever beheld its acres
No being, I surmise, has ever felt its depth
Filtered through the eyes of the Eldritch in darkness opaque
And yet I find my fear bereft;
I deem him dim who moves in shadow
While with arrow nocked stalks the shapeless
But is himself perhaps the prey tomorrow
With soul devoid of that fear formless;
Certainly I am not so blessed, for I have failed where I sought to prevail—and was destroyed
So I became material and peered through the veil—and I conquered the Void

## Sonnet of Time

A place my dreams always see creation
Where your welded words never guide the sun
There is peace in my imagination
But why my good God do you grant me none?
Although patience makes the deepest dreams blossom
Change calls for currency, I am its cash
Each moment is so quickly forgotten
Night breaks to daylight and day burns to ash;
Future turns to present, years become months
What more can I give—all of me is wrung
You had it all, and still you stole my sum
Left me with nothing but the starting gun;
If dream is your bait, if Time is your trap
Then your grace is the noose wrapped

## Sonnet of Belief

There are times when you believe I lie
And yet I have not died and shall not die;
There are times when Death stalks the dignified
And I look upon the glimmer in his eye;
He creeps up from behind and I swallow, terrified
But I need not brace, for that is not my label:
I am no one noble, I am no hero from fable
I am not the sort who dies outside, but every night on the inside
I would not deny myself a coward; I would not argue with this truth
But if you still believe me not, think that these are all lies—
That I am someone dignified, then see me indignant
I am the one in the paintings, I am the one forever aloof:
For if not, then let me be Death’s fruit

## Sonnet of Hate

Kneel before me and I shall take your head
For I hate you, my friend, like life hates death
When you write your will, I will be your lead
You fail at fathoming my hatred's depths;
We finally sit across in the booth
I turn to you: your blue eyes, both untrue
See through my scope: glimpse my golden gaze’s truth!
Know I will see to it that you die too;
Over all these years that I have hated
The more I gaze, the more I see a smile!
Tell me if all these years have been wasted—
Has that look been genuine all the while?
I suppose it is alike life and death
For is it not death which gives life its depth?

## Sonnet of the Skeptic

And at times I take two as two
I do not ponder if it may be one;
I think truth is to truth
As moon is to sun;
I take this as that
And thus I am done;
But then I think, times have seen me over too soon
And so I run, and I run and I run!—but I shall never take two as two
For if this is true, if two is two, if moon is moon — then my time is far too soon
My days are over! oh! of this life so bleak; so my eyes roll back and I fall into a dream
And that unconscious that rules me—me, my fate; that person who rues—me, fable and fake
At times I crumble, at times I weep, but at others I think of lies and their dreams
So I will not take two as two — I will never take moon as moon
I will never take truth as truth —
I will never take two as two

## Sonnet of Me

There on the day that I dabble in hate
When the time I dread grows ever so near
And take to hating my accursed fate
I want to speak, but there is only fear;
I say I should not dread the dreadful day
And yet I cannot stop myself thinking
Of all my doubted, counted minutes, say
I will never stop this soulless drinking;
Realize the worthless worth of my word
Understand the depthless depth of my hate
Fate shall see me slowly fused with the herd
See that me and myself do bear this trait;
When I have said all that I shall so say
Bloodstain shall lead to my wrathful decay

## Sonnet of Dust

Born from the ash of incarnation
Realized from the earth indefinite
I can only try to repeat verbatim
But you shall weep and proclaim “I did it!”;
So I shall seek the dust of dawn
And never leave like the early morn
My days dissolve in fumes now gone
I watch the days play out like the storms;
I think there is nothing so surreal
As that infinity which I wield
Upon that shimmering silvery ideal
Cursing all my days, yet never once kneeled;
I think to have spawned from that earthen crust,
I think I was born from the roaring dust

## Sonnet of Ambatukam

O God, am I hated by pleasure?
The sweetest Ambrosia aflame,
There is nothing so great a treasure!
And my hand shall speak the same;
It shall burst like the pyres
Amid the shouting of lies,
The most brilliant ivory fire
Bright as the glorious cries;
Then, like the almighty seer of old,
My sword shall grow the length of an abode,
Point at times when Man knew nothing of gold
And then I shall groan and unleash my load;
I think I may dream, down deep in my soul
Into pleasure I plunge, like a dream-bull

## Sonnet of Renewal

There has never been a thing born anew
As that dipped in Elysium pool
Dripping from the bath of the godly few
While uncaring Fate spins its timeless spool;
By letter I have received a summoning
From the haughty Olympus crew
I hear the heavenly hymns a-humming
Why, I shall meet them bathed anew;
Zeus and his pride ignite a fire in my eyes
I shall pluck them like olives, as men do
Athena addresses me smooth in a sigh:
“Why do you lust for all our godly truth?”
Shall I be like Prometheus, that old fool
Or make for the heavens, a mortal renewed?

## Sonnet of Inheritance

Once Man was meant for many things
Like conquering the vast blue
But their nature is ever fleeting
And so the cycle of killing ensues;
Twice wrestled from their own maw
Man and his way may demonstrate order
In the waiting days approaching dawn
But fools shall seek disorder,
So numbers dwindle quick
Man slips into that abyss
And Society shuns the sciences
If only they knew the risks!
The day that Man grasps the distant Mars,
Ex terra, ad astra—They shall inherit the stars!

## Sonnet of Revenge

There is nothing so sweet as revenge, I deem
And I would stake my heart upon this feat
That he who says revenge is not a worthwhile dream
Is but a child who has never tasted defeat;
But in a man who thinks lust is sin
Yet claims desire is totally different
Preaching good over evil to Him
But is himself not that innocent;
I would not call him a liar, only misguided
To ignore love in disregarding hate
A fate lying in a realm divided
Suppose he never learned to be defiant;
On those souls who have caused you strife,
Know that there is no err in taking their life

## Sonnet of Destination

That which is always the destination
Hears no mention of my descending
Has no watch to witness creation
And bears no sadness of end approaching;
But to rebuke all the claims upon me
All but evident in the days that we share
Should the day fog overcome our glee
So shall it be made into Nightmare;
I see across the distant lands
I rest atop the sky-eating mountains
Yet Time, in approaching, ran its hands
While Destiny, in the distance, kicked up the sands;
Yet my heart on this journey will only stop once,
So, I say, a life lived fully should never feel like mere months

## Sonnet of Sand

Do not act as though you know the desert
Do not think you have ever felt so sore
Until you have walked upon the sands
Under the foxy sun’s orange gore;
And what their flaming embers wrought
Be that beside the painful grains
Imagine if you truly knew the sands
You would be as I am — insane;
Yet I still think quietly, some days
While I rest upon the warm sand
Whether things could have seen change
Or has Time’s curse not lifted from this land?
But these thoughts die, dispelled from my mind,
For everything turns to sand, given Time

## Sonnet of War

There are things in motion above
Where the world moves on unhindered
The skies are filled with love
But the ground is full of ash and cinder;
Families torn apart at the seams
Good is nothing that gleams with soot
Men die without a single dream
Some call it serene, but they are the root;
I think humanity has never seen tragedy
As that born from War
Civilian life felled by fallen amnesty
Death is the rich man’s payment to the poor;
They say he that rises in human defense, is he the truly evil,
For speaking such nonsense against Them, men not his equal

## Sonnet of Chaos

In the depths of nightmare and severed ties
Whispered fear turns to tears, signifying loss
Where the dark of night dyes my eyes
Deep in the yawning maw of Chaos;
No legend ever stood the test of time
No myth ever led to the decline of faith
So when began the hate-borne crime
All claimed it was the machinations of fate;
I thank the stars that grace infinity's border
For bestowing me freedom from the laws
When Chaos birthed order from disorder
And reason turned tangible my flaws;
Once, all knew why light fled the sky long ago,
Now only I know of darkness’ eternal flow

## Sonnet of Paradox

I know Paradox is perpetuated eternally
Light covets shadow yet thinks itself bright
Night is the shining moon burning morbidly
Day is the desolate blight when the sky ignites;
I hope yesterday comes tomorrow
Such that I regret my ways
But night finds my happiness borrowed
By fair sleep and her shade;
Where a man alone loves it
He, too, hates the thing he loves
And he is not without his wit
Maybe without merit, crying at the release of dove;
Say you know what Paradox is, say you know it well,
Say you know the depth of contradiction, how only time will tell

## Sonnet of Fear

Remedy of the heart is something I realize
In the light of midnight, where darkness presides
Testing the waters of life's grand size
Doing as men do and taking two sides;
But my eyes are windows to wells down under
Of cavernous depths in halls sundered above
Portraying itself in a call which makes us wonder
Sealing my memory in the tomb of my lover;
O Death, do take me as you have courage!
Take me to places dark as silent dread
Make me hear till I'm sore and aged
To places I'd fear for my helpless head;
As in the tales that speak of your ways,

## Sonnet of the Fog

There is a veil my thoughts cannot penetrate
A cloud billowing in my tired mind
Sending signals of anxiety, I cannot wait
The day I realize the fog is right behind;
Now, I say, death would be a blessing
I try to wear my skin as sin atoned
Yet here is me, in death, confessing
For reasons unknown, I cannot think alone;
Say can the day ever be felled
Where it turns to night, yet I remain sane?
Syllables find me pressed upon myself
Wearied by my own mental strain;
Tell me if the Fog in stalking me for all these years,
Obscured the fear lingering when I peered in the mirror

## Sonnet of Melody

In my ears there rings a soothing melody
As if brought upon by my daydreams
Rooted in lost mind and forgotten memory
Reminding myself all is not as it seems;
And might I deem it sufficient in breath
To waste my words on lowly folk
Who cower from my brethren dressed
All in white and standing in fancy meadows;
Fatigue, again, is restless in my heart
And in memory joy departs, yet I see some
Whistling tunes penned by muses of the arts
I write now not for mere amusement but freedom;
Freedom from all that harms my mind,
Freedom I would sooner leave behind

## Sonnet of Retrieval

I recall the day destiny drew forth
And laid a blade at my feet
I knew not what it was for
But I had a guess it was for me;
There, in the depths of night,
And, here, I should say
Is where I lost my sight
I say it was here I misplaced my way;
Down in dusk and clad in dust
I take to memory that day I won
The day everything seemed so grand
When that sword weighed but a pound and I was not shunned;
I cannot count my blessings on a single hand,
But I can the days since my sword has tasted man

## Sonnet of the Trap

I, who have grappled with sadness,
Find solace in delusion
In places of frenzy and madness
I pray for absolution;
Now death is a dark destrier
Galloping in the nightly cold
Singing songs of dried tears
Telling me I am old;
He is Death, that dark destrier,
Whose ancient trap sees ages unfold
Having caged me untold years
As he gallops in the nightly cold;
Who claims I have woven his story from flesh into sin,
Into fabric again, the seams press upon my skin

## Sonnet of the Damned

## Sonnet of Tranquility

In the halls of placid lakes
Where fantasy breeds reality
Imagination remakes the fake
And takes us on nascent journeys;
When falsity calls for pending removal
Sentimentality can be a virtue
And shall truth be deemed final
Judgment’s gavel pays their dues;
I may say in the midst of superstition
Resting in that holy mission’s care
That my soul craves a lengthy session
To abate this sorrowful Nightmare;
In this placid place that lacks disaster,
Can I say my days have never been happier?

## Sonnet of Hunger

Shall hunger find me penniless
Stomach growling, never abating

The true weight of China plating;

Can I count on hated Fate?
Meat rare as rainbow hanging raw

Dripping blood from my grinning maw;
Would they see me as something different,
If I bled blue blood and golden liquid?

## Sonnet of Solemnity

Solemnity unbroken by annoyance
Is a thing I shudder to even wonder
In the tomb of the dead god's trance
Where the mother of stillness slumbers;
So I ponder on that solemn owl
While it sleeps, shrouded in oaky leaves,
Coated with black paint and a quiet cowl
On deep, night-wrenched eves;
Bred from placidity, an owl unburdened
By what wakes at night's emergence,
Breathing solemnity, serious and certain,
Unbothered by worldly disturbance;
Is it so, then, that the night has been weaved,
From the sitting, flitting fowl’s solemn dreams?

Find me in no other place, than its cold and icy embrace

## Sonnet of Remembrance

Standing before me in silence,
Monuments of stalwart indifference
Zealous eyes drowning in dependence
Lingering with fake reverence;
I am made jealous by their presence
At what could have been my own
Caught in the ghost of repentance
My voice comes out in a hollow tone:
“Fiends and savages, statues in my tomb,
Monuments of a religious cure
Dependent on a virgin mother’s womb
You seek me! — I am salvation assured;
Yet I am no different, no less belligerent,
I am not your remedy, nor a medicine
Coated in clean Remembrance,
I am but a man — my head is sin;”
Watching me zealously, rows of statues in view,
Here I stand then, before the hallowed pews

## Sonnet of Connection

What but dusk begins at dawn?
It rebounds in the early hours
But when the wick’s flame is gone
Light burns in dark’s shower;
What but light covets the early dark?
Except that praised with high acclaim
But of otherwise low remark
Tell me if these two can be the same?
But when the winds cease and die
And the land is mild throughout
See clear skies break to storm-eyes
And the rhythm of rain run out;
There is nothing in this world without its other part,
All is bound by some Connection, some quantum counterpart

## Sonnet of the Sonnetless

I pity the dead poet
Whose words met death unheard
And the notebook he wrote in, forgotten
As his legacy is merged with the herd;
So now that he is lifeless
Dressed in Death and abolished
He is to be Sonnetless
All his Sonnets quietly demolished;
Shall I pity the old fool?
Who thought his work inadequate
Less a poet, more a fool,
At the mercy of his fearful wit;
Pity him, the poet sleeping lifeless,
Now honest in death, yet forever Sonnetless

## Sonnet of Silence

Dear sky entombed in miserable gray
An assailant of the desolate shade
A player in your silent parade
You have swayed me with your ways;
For not indulging into you
I feel as though I am a fool
Succumbing to your cool abuse
And submitting to your soothing rule;
Having given me your silent pity
Sitting silently, clad in calamity,
Your gray eye is cruel and silencing
Seducing me independently;
Now bury me in Silence, so that I forever lie,
Silently entombed beneath overcast skies

## Sonnet of the Exception

Born to a mortal womb
I am forever cursed with this mortal wound
Where impending loss forever looms
The tomb of time is accursed doom;
By accepting the unacceptable
I scoffed in the face of your pity
And rewrit the impossibility
Of death’s inevitability;
Ever since my accursed birth
I have searched for power of the divine sort
Scouring the earth and all its dirt
In search of some immortal warmth;
A mortal who muted the immutable,
I am the Exception to the ineffable

## Sonnet of the Rapture

Why do I not hear the sonorous chords?
Is justice deaf? Has silence befallen Death's church?
No! O would-be lords clawing at Death’s door,
Slave-like masses, monsters craving Death's cure:
Know that I, alone, have seen the barren land of absence—
Whereby, practicing abstinence, his eminence will adore me!
And praise my Folly by entreating me entrance
To the realm of dead sentiment and silent pity;
So rupture my core in your Rapture — see it stretched, sore, and shattered,
Awash in weakness, but a mortal man — O savior of mine, weather this strife!
Age me in my thoughts—wrought my rot out from under me—
For I am laden in a certain misery—the misery of life;
Pardon my birth upon your breast, O deity of Death,
I would die at your request, concede my final breath

## Sonnet of Darkness

I swim in the abyssal sea
Crawling among the lightless deep
Through the sea grove and coral reef
The Reaper’s scythe hanging above me;
The menace of Darkness is meritless!
Yes, it is all Fantasia—feared, were it reality,
Yet, it is but the dreams of the dreamless—
Not my own! Bred of an incessant quality;
Still, I am unable to shake the thought—though I try—
Of the depth of my fearborn inadequacy
For it is death’s determination to see me die
That has left my soul shattered inside;
O empty menace, my soul made lifeless,
Wholly at the mercy of my darling, Darkness

## Sonnet of the Faultless

In a sense, nothing is so futile
As to find fault in the Faultless
In fulfillment of your own insecurities
Blaming the blameless relentlessly;
Nothing is to be shunned, then,
So much as it is to be undone
And bent to the wills of imbecilic men
Of who Folly finds no fault beneath the sun;
But Fortune shall devour! O the chance shall shine
And breathe life into despoiled lungs,
O wretched past and ruined future!
You gleam lusterlessly before my eyes;
By my borrowed sentence — say, crave you restless pestilence?
O present! That restless presence! Return to essence, and pay penance to the Faultless

## Sonnet of the Sinner

If, in a man's haste, he finds
His hands nailed to a dying sign
What little life he lived defined
By the sins wrought upon his kind;
And of what he thought of death:
What little life lied in his eye
If ever dormant, he'd measure the breadth,
And proclaim himself the most significant;
As in the minds of the pious,
Morality is fallacy in immoral minds—
Caught by the grave and vanquished,
Hiding pity inside self-righteous eyes;
Serenity through sin redeemed sacrificially,
Is no less artificial than promised eternity

## Sonnet of Circumstance

Wrought upon the world-stretched shore
The wretched sand craves me endlessly
And I find myself at such an impasse
As to warrant my drowning sanity;
Alone at the edge of the burning ocean,
I've learned to crave the placid sea
As in the fantastic dreams of fanatics
Shall my earthly captor set me free;
Yet I know such words betray my state
This Circumstance I find myself facing,

## Sonnet of the Ancients

Oh love’s chance to love another
And hold fast to them for dire comfort
There is nothing quite like romance
But the infinite pleasure of finite desire;
As in a liar, who knows little of love
To love another is purely of
Such a fail of face, a mistake,
One may say it is insane;
And still, I hurt against the past
When I look back and see my love
Watching me from the Heaven that stole her
I recall I am not so long from her as to hurt;
For I am no youth whose life is young,
But an aged man with an Ancient love

## Sonnet of the Tangible

In the realm of the intangible
I seek some semblance of control,
That Death, in his mortal form,
Would form in me a soul;
But I know not my state:
Should I shiver in his grip,
Stranded in the dying cold
Beholden to nothingness;
There I grasp the unforgiving phantom!
And hold him fast, that he would not permit
Me some feeble recompense
For each hope he deemed unfit;
In a ghostly hope for some unlit wish,
I hope for nothing less than Tangible flesh

## Sonnet of the Stars

The lonely expanse of space surrounds me,
Stars flickering faintly in the distance
Eternal, they yearn endlessly
For some semblance of redemption;
Breathless, helpless to their sentence,
I witness their struggle against the sky
And cannot help but shudder hence,
When light surrenders to darkness;
And so little is as radiant
As the sky that dies above me,
Rays burning at the height of day
Desperately searching for salvation;
In grieving silence, tears fill my somber eyes,
Watching dying Stars scorch the skyline

## Sonnet of Sorrow

Naught is quite as incredulous
As the raspy grasp of death,
Its clawed hand reminiscent
Of Sorrows departed;
While inside I cry my life away,
And ask into the dying night
That if dawn be luminescent
Why, for all its holy light,
Does it not chase despondent dusk
From where that darkness hides?
If it be so incessantly appealing
To all manners of appetite
I ask, therefore, how the dying wind,
Can serve to please my thousand sins?

## Sonnet of the Legion

The lesion of darkness is legionless
With its poisonous inheritance
Splendors wasted on martyrs,
Death married in resplendence;
Horrid hate is coveted
Like the second coming,
Christ is alive in death
Love is not so blessed;
Hypocrites, protestors
Marching on through bad weather
As though it is not venom
Seeping in through the silence;
The Legion storms the castle walls,
Their swords aimless as they fall

## Sonnet of the Crow

In vain I have sought, not found,
Amid my search for penance,
Sought to hear a sound
Repeating with a cadence;
A song with a stark presence
Whose tone against the noise
Is an unmistakable cadence
Cawed out with great poise;
And the thought of a Crow
Perched in the lightless abbey
Watching from the silent row
At my movements, at me;
Dead is the deep night’s fowl,
Starved of darkness and its cowl

## Sonnet of Death

Restless is the grave of Death
When all that is unholy,
Wanders upon the accursed earth,
Dead and searching breathlessly;
Poisoned is its aching,
And so dreadful is the sound
Of a thousand fingers pulling
Reaching at the soil of the ground;
And so profane is the wound
From which sin, seeping through:
Into corpses, migrating—
A parasite of evil;
This act, which some have criminalized,
Is but a single symptom of suicide

## Sonnet of Salvation

To keep a sanguine devil sane,
Fictional reality is its grace,
And truth is a damnable liar
Bleeding on the hunter's stake;
What worth are your pains?
Your life may yet be saved,
Or burned in the flames
Of forgotten Salvation;
Let burning hatred
Keep your earthly soul awake,
Away from temptation,
Free and untamed;
In every heaven minds have conjured,
Death is the only fear ever pondered

## Sonnet of the Wind

The Wind howling in the silence:
From dying wails, turn to ice
Great idols fall to violence,
To the infinite excise;
Where quiet voices whisper low
Of haunted figures dancing fast
Inside halls of twisting bows,
Man's insanity outlasts;
There eyes are incensed,
Ears to their voices listen
Until they speak, their speech incessant,
Outpouring words of hated sentiment;
Where Wind has sent their lies defiant,
False oracles stand in silence
