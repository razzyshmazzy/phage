---
title: "The Devil is an Angel"
date: 2026-07-28
category: poetry
---

I
I stand before my reflection,
before a man I dare not kill.
I am before my master,
like Moses on the hill.
On my knees I pray
for perpetual repentance.
II
God, who am I?
Not your blessed creation,
not so everlasting.
Perhaps I am this wretch,
this condemnable heretic?
I cannot be loved, be saved!
Fastened to this unjust punishment,
I am parched and pathless;
The moon flies away
across a nebulous sky,
waxing and waning and
devoid of direction;
In the misty shroud forming
on the crematory horizon,
I watch it arc into the safety
of a promised neverland;
And surely eastward lying,
in that Marxist blizzard
are Angels who will ferry us
through soot and circumstance
to that promised neverland—
Is this that holy promise,
or another lie?
Is it even you up there
in Heaven, watching,
or is it some impostor?
I cannot quench this feeling,
though I try to stave it off,
that our Lord in Heaven is absent,
that His kingdom has been seized
by some demon
whose Heaven
is our Hell.
III
Dwelling deep within the earth,
rising from out the dripping magma,
from out the boiling spring,
he smiles, pulls up a chair,
and sits with us for supper.
IV
For the Devil does not dwell
in some imaginary gaol,
in some intangible hell,
but walks and
has walked
amongst us,
every aching century;
Striding beside us,
subsuming our planet
through violence;
And, beneath our notice,
he has since found his place
on the earth which we inhabit
which Hell has since replaced.
V
Does Heaven still stand
as our earth stands still?
What, with the Devil here,
it must be in ruin;
For as hard as it is to fathom
the luridity of the thing,
it is no less, recalling hence,
that your actions are done
in reverence;
Such is only possible
if all the angels and saints
have fallen from that lofty heaven
and are rotting in their graves.
VI
When free will absconds
from out our mortal hearts,
its cause is obvious:
what else could affect this,
but the absence of our Lord
in Heaven?
And in his absence
from that idyllic heaven,
I find myself marching
forward without purpose;
marching forward,
strength stirred
by the butt of a rifle
biting sharply
into my back;
Not questioning
the reason we march,
but suspecting the
steel machines in the skies;
to an end seldom
worthwhile,
to a place drunker
with death and deceit
than ever witnessed
by the guiltless;
Survival is only found
in the recesses of one’s mind;
trapped within some dream
is the only form of solace
that I can find,
imagining some
better place
less bitter with pain.
VII
If the Lord in Heaven
is truly gone—
Then what sense is there
in continual existence
without the essential liberty?
There is no life without liberty,
for what else is there to live?
I thought I knew my humanity;
I thought I knew the good, the pious,
but I was wrong;
It is said in the texts:
“The Devil dons the evilest disguise,”
and, having met him,
I can say this much is true,
for I have seen him in his multitude,
seen him in his glory; seen
him laughing and crying,
pouring chlorine gas
into the lungs
of little ones.
VIII
There is no disguise as wretched or evil
as the skin and flesh of humanity,
the most sinful of all animals—
among every creature of the earth.
IX
One evening as I beheld
my reflection in the mirror
and held my son in one palm,
what was left of him at least,
most of him slipped
through the gaps in my
shaking fingers
and onto the floor; it was then,
amid my sorrow, when
suddenly the sky darkened
and shadow fell over the world;
I felt the breath
of a ceaseless hunger
on my neck;
The call of death
stressed my soul,
made restless my heart,
dousing the dregs
of hope
with dread;
In that void I heard
the voice of a survivor
an unutterable horror,
a mix of gargling terror
a million caged screams
forever stranded
in the aether;
Conforming slowly
to the dim light,
a shadow darted by
a shudder passed me by;
His proportions,
normal and average,
maintained the human façade;
seeing it, evoked in me
a fear I can hardly suffocate,
for its quality, so deep
and inexorably terrifying,
made a mockery
of my soul;
No second chance,
I turned away
without a second glance,
for the features
which he wore,
still as grotesque
as biblical prophets,
might drive a saner man
insane;
Despite this—still I turned,
looked up to see,
a solitary figure
standing in the sea
of cold and wintry
darkness;
The fog of death veiled him,
hung around him like a shroud;
He seemed to me then
some grossly clad villain
draped in the Devil’s black;
Sometimes he acted as though
the angel’s white he wore
when he pretended
to be a doctor,
made each life he took
all the more
deserving;
Of course, the truth
was different,
far removed from
such a mind,
so far so
clad in bias.
X
Before me he appeared,
seeming to be
the Reaper, who strangely
was not grim, and rather oozed
a sort of chilling happiness,
sick upon his smiling
countenance;
Disguised in human tissue,
I saw the Devil as he paced
the perimeter at Auschwitz—
I watched him from afar,
from the safety of the distance,
at least I thought as much
until, all of a sudden,
he whipped around
and spun; hideous fear
sunk into me;
In that moment I sought refuge,
though here, I knew,
there was no refuge;
At last, I dove and hid
in conscious futility,
when suddenly his boots
strode up beside me; I felt his presence
immediately behind me;
Thankfully,
I was hidden—which
was rather foolish, considering
Death is seldom inaccurate;
I turned around hesitantly; and I
found him staring; it shook me to see
such a hideous gaze
staring back at me;
This Angel of Death, looking down at me,
his eyes dissecting me
in my entirety; I knew
I was beyond salvation;
At last I heard him; his voice,
kind and consoling, granted me solace,
as it had my son before me; I waited
patiently in my own embrace; his hand,
reaching out,
suddenly fell upon me—Evil Incarnate
dug his claws deep into me;
Suddenly possessed
by a fear so deep and insidious,
I had before lied
when I said I knew pain,
for I had never known something
so hideous,
for I had not yet known him.
XI
Instead of seeking ends
by means of empathy,
we manufacture
our own convergence
toward the most artificial evil,
toward the end of empathy;
For that is the aim of man,
that is what we do,
sacrificing ourselves
to Eugenics;
As a result of our hubris
humanity has cast itself
into a mold of its own making.
XII
Society is a pot filled with sin;
Goodness retreating into the abyss,
every sin accumulates—the pot
shall not hold much longer, not when
the Devil’s spoon stirs it,
not when human hands
guide it.
Boiling on the stove,
each sin is blended
in with the rest;
Good nor evil is clear
in this entropic cascade;
In this monstrous façade
that threatens society,
as if society has not
already
threatened itself;
And finally, it culminates:
the result
is Evil Incarnate;
But even
in the purest alloy,
there are still impurities;
Even gleaming gold
is criticized; perfection
is impossible;
Even in this state,
goodness still exists;
however muffled
or diluted
that goodness may be,
the fact is
it is there,
and that is enough—
that is constant,
that is true.
XIII
And, though it’s dim,
I think I see a yellow star
cutting thinly through the darkness—
And, though it’s faint,
I hear that yellow star,
beating cacophonous
in my ears, on my breast,
in my heart; then in the west,
I see the rope in my cell—I covet it,
and move my hand to grab it—
Oh, what a fiendish thing to do!
To succumb to this—
was I really so weak
all along?
Really so weak the mere presence
of the Devil and of death
has made me seek salvation
through such a wretched end?
Unable to withstand the burden,
the crematorium before me,
I have stooped
to such a lowly state—
And oh, what a wretch I am.
A product of my birth?
Surely, that is all I am.
But you will lie and say
it’s blasphemy,
which I somewhat understand;
For who could condemn
their fellow man
if he considered him
as human as himself?
Worry not; in your hell,
I know myself;
I am confident
in my survival;
For no place is as hellish,
no place is as vile
as the hell which exists
on this rift between worlds;
that ravages this limbo,
that preys on our souls;
And if Heaven
is really
and truly
in ruins,
then where else could Hell be
but amongst our cities?
XIV
Why do so many living souls
walk happily beside the Devil?
